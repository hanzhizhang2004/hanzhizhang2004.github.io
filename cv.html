Research Interests

My research interests lie in the theory–data interface of gravitational-wave physics. On the data side, I want to use gravitational-wave and other observations to infer the properties of black holes or test fundamental physics.

Research Experience and Projects

Relativistic Mean-Motion Resonance under Self-Force details → 10.2026 – present
Tsinghua University · Research Assistant · Supervisor: Prof. Huan Yang
  • Studying the resonant structure of two small particles orbiting a black hole under gravitational self-force theory, aiming to predict the gravitational-wave signatures of mean-motion resonances.
  • Derived the equations of motion for the resonant angle Ψ under the bodies' self-forces and mutual interaction. At leading order they reduce to a tilted pendulum, Ψ̈ = εΛ0 + 2ε ∑p (αVp sin pΨ + Λp cos pΨ).
  • Using time-reversal parity, identified each term as self or mutual, and conservative or dissipative. Showed that the conservative part of the dynamics is Hamiltonian.
  • Obtained all coefficients from gravitational-wave energy fluxes via black-hole perturbation theory, without metric reconstruction or regularisation, and computed phase portraits to identify the resonant structure.
  • Next: subleading order (capture into and escape from resonance), gravitational-wave signatures, and Kerr.
Gravitational Self-Force in Black Hole Scattering details → 07.2026 – 10.2026
Remote · Supervised by Prof. Leor Barack, with Prof. Niels Warburton et al. · Paper in preparation
  • Studied the even-parity dipole metric perturbation of a particle on a hyperbolic orbit around a Schwarzschild black hole. In vacuum it is pure gauge, generated on each side of the worldline by a gauge vector, and it carries the centre-of-mass (CoM) freedom. I focused on its behaviour at large radii.
  • Proved that, among globally regular gauge vectors, the entire CoM freedom is carried by two gauge modes: a static one that shifts the CoM, and one linear in time that boosts it.
  • Proved uniqueness within a polynomial-in-time ansatz: fixing the CoM gives the unique solution.
  • By solving for bases of gauge vectors on both sides of the worldline and matching them through the jump conditions across it, I constructed the solution in closed form and showed that it must be linear in time. This solution is exact for a particle at infinity, with corrections of relative order M/rp at finite radius.
  • Reconstructed the metric perturbation to provide initial data for the collaborators' numerical simulations.
Gravitational-Wave Searches with Pulsar Timing Arrays details → 06.2025 – 09.2025
Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics (AEI) · Supervisor: Dr. Rutger van Haasteren

PTAs detect a gravitational-wave background through cross-correlated timing residuals. Dr. van Haasteren derived the optimal quadratic statistic and its p-value in the time domain. Gamma-ray pulsars lack reliable arrival times, so my project moved the pipeline to the Fourier domain.

  • Derived the optimal detection statistic in terms of Fourier coefficients alone, and reduced its dimension by projecting onto the low-rank support of the quadratic filter (thin SVD).
  • Computed p-values from the generalised χ2 null distribution (Imhof's method), averaged over MCMC posterior samples of the noise hyperparameters.
  • Implemented the prior covariance φ directly in the Fourier domain, removing the dependence on nanograv/enterprise (validated against it), so the whole pipeline is Fourier-native.
  • Applied the Python pipeline to 35 gamma-ray pulsars and found no significant Hellings–Downs correlation.
Optimising Diamagnetic Levitation details → 07.2024 – 09.2024
University College London · Supervisor: Prof. Peter Barker
  • Studied how to shape a diamagnetic body so that it levitates highest above a given permanent magnet, or oscillates horizontally fastest. I used a cylindrical core with an oppositely magnetised shell as the example.
  • Computed the magnetic field from the magnetic scalar potential, obtained by a Green's-function integral over the magnet.
  • Constructed vertical force and horizontal frequency densities, whose volume integrals give the magnetic lift and the oscillation frequency.
  • Solved both shape optimisations with Lagrange multipliers: the highest-floating shape is a lens-shaped ring above the core–shell junction, the fastest-oscillating one a ring hugging the magnet's outer edge.
Multi-Body Problem and Lagrangian Points 04.2024
University College London · Undergraduate Coursework · Project examiner: Prof. David Bowler
  • Used numerical methods to solve the stable two-body and three-body gravitational problems, including the Earth–Moon–Sun system, with Velocity-Verlet and fourth-order Runge–Kutta methods.
  • Analysed the accuracy and stability of the Velocity-Verlet and fourth-order Runge–Kutta methods.
  • Proved that there are five Lagrangian points and analysed the stability of each.
  • Investigated how the mass ratios affect the stability.
