Research Interests

My research interests lie broadly in general relativity, gravitational physics and black holes.

  • Gravitational-wave physics and astrophysics. Theoretical modelling of gravitational-wave sources and gravitational dynamics, including gravitational self-force and numerical relativity, as well as data analysis in dark energy and gravitational-wave astronomy.
  • General relativity. Mathematical relativity, black-hole uniqueness, gravitational instantons and twistor theory.
  • Black holes. Quasinormal modes and ringdowns, AdS black holes and higher-dimensional black holes.

Research Experience and Projects

Relativistic Mean-Motion Resonance 10.2026 – present
Tsinghua University · Research Assistant · Supervisor: Prof. Huan Yang
  • Derived first-order equations of motion for resonant angles in double extreme-mass-ratio inspirals using gravitational self-force theory.
  • Proved that the conservative dynamics are generated by a Hamiltonian and derived that Hamiltonian.
  • Analysed capture into mean-motion resonance in the presence of radiation reaction.
  • Applying black-hole perturbation theory and metric reconstruction to compute orbital evolution and identify gravitational-wave signatures of resonance.
Gravitational Self-Force in Black Hole Scattering 06.2026 – 10.2026
Remote · Supervised by Prof. Leor Barack · In collaboration with Prof. Niels Warburton and others
  • Studied even-parity dipole metric perturbations in Schwarzschild spacetime within gravitational self-force theory, focusing on asymptotic solutions at spatial infinity for hyperbolic orbits.
  • Expressed metric-perturbation amplitudes in terms of harmonic components of gauge vectors. Derived their vacuum equations away from the particle's worldline and the jumps in their values and derivatives across it.
  • Proved existence and uniqueness of the linear-in-time solution satisfying the vacuum equations and jump conditions after fixing the system's centre of mass.
  • Constructed explicit bases of vacuum solutions and determined their coefficients analytically on both sides of the particle's trajectory.
  • Proved by construction that exactly two modes change the centre of mass, one its position and the other its velocity. Both belong to the linear-in-time solution.
  • Reconstructed the metric perturbations and their initial time derivatives to provide Cauchy data for numerical evolution.
Gravitational-Wave Searches Using Pulsar Timing Arrays 06.2025 – 09.2025
Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics (AEI) · Supervisor: Dr. Rutger van Haasteren

Pulsar timing arrays search for gravitational waves through cross-correlations in the timing residuals of different pulsars. I extended Dr. van Haasteren's time-domain framework for optimal hypothesis testing of these correlations to the Fourier domain.

  • Derived the PTA detection statistic in the Fourier domain and reduced the dimension of its quadratic filter matrix.
  • Evaluated posterior-predictive p-values via MCMC using generalised χ2 distributions.
  • Implemented the prior covariance φ in the Fourier basis, removing the dependency on nanograv/enterprise, and validated equivalence with enterprise.
  • Implemented the complete Fourier-domain pipeline in Python and applied it to gamma-ray data. GitHub repository.
Optimising Diamagnetic Levitation 07.2024 – 09.2024
University College London · Supervisor: Prof. Peter Barker
  • Developed a numerical method to find the shape that maximises the levitation height of a diamagnetic material above a rotationally symmetric permanent magnet. Tested it on a uniformly magnetised cylindrical core surrounded by an oppositely magnetised cylindrical shell.
  • Analysed the stability and dynamics of the levitated material and computed the shape that maximises its horizontal oscillation frequency.
Multi-Body Problem and Lagrangian Points 04.2024
University College London · Undergraduate Coursework · Examiner: Prof. David Bowler
  • Used Velocity-Verlet and fourth-order Runge–Kutta to solve two-body and three-body gravitational problems, including the Earth–Moon–Sun system.
