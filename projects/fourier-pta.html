Fourier-Domain Quadratic Statistics in Pulsar Timing Arrays

Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics (AEI)  ·  Supervised by Dr. Rutger van Haasteren

The optimal-statistic framework for pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) provides a fast, model-agnostic detection statistic for stochastic gravitational-wave backgrounds. Its calibration — and the assignment of $p$-values to observed values — has so far been formulated in the time domain. This project extends that machinery to the Fourier domain, in line with recent work on Fourier-space PTA likelihoods.

The work was both theoretical and computational: I derived the Fourier-domain detection statistic, reduced its quadratic filter matrix, computed analytic background distributions for $p$-values via generalised $\chi^2$ distributions, and implemented the resulting pipeline in Python, freed of any dependence on nanograv/enterprise.

The detection statistic

In the time domain, with white-noise covariance $\widetilde{W}$ (after marginalising the timing-model parameters) and Fourier design matrix $F$, the residuals have covariance

$$ N = \widetilde{W} + F\,\phi_N\,F^\top, \qquad C = \widetilde{W} + F\,\phi_C\,F^\top, $$

under the null and alternative hypotheses respectively. The standard quadratic filter is

$$ Q \;=\; \frac{N^{-1}(C-N)\,N^{-1}}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[\,N^{-1}(C-N)\,N^{-1}(C-N)\,\big]}}. $$

Changing basis via $\Sigma^{-1} = F^\top \widetilde{W}^{-1} F + \phi_N^{-1}$ and $\hat{a} = \Sigma\, F^\top \widetilde{W}^{-1}\,\delta t$, repeated application of the Woodbury identity and completion of the square reduces the unnormalised numerator to a strikingly compact form:

$$ \hat{a}^{\top} Q'_f \,\hat{a} \;=\; \hat{a}^{\top}\, \phi_N^{-1}\,(\phi_C - \phi_N)\,\phi_N^{-1}\,\hat{a}. $$

The normalisation factor simplifies likewise, and the full Fourier-domain filter reads

$$ Q_{\text{Fourier}} \;=\; \frac{\phi_N^{-1}\,\Delta\phi\,\phi_N^{-1}}{\sqrt{\,2\,\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\,\bigl((\Sigma^{-1}-\phi_N^{-1})\,\Delta\phi\,\phi_N^{-1}\Sigma\bigr)^{2}\,\right]\,}}, $$

with $\Delta\phi = \phi_C - \phi_N$. Notably the singular object $\widetilde{W}$ never appears in the final expression — only its inverse, which is what is well-defined in any case.

Dimension reduction

Because cross-correlations involve only a subset of Fourier coefficients, $\Delta\phi$ is low-rank. Writing the projector onto that subspace as $P = G G^\top$ and performing a thin QR decomposition $\phi_N^{-1} G = G_A R$, the statistic collapses to a low-dimensional form

$$ \mathcal{D} \;=\; \frac{y^\top \Phi_N\, \Delta\Phi\, \Phi_N^\top y}{\sqrt{\,2\,\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[(D_r\, C_r\, \Delta\Phi)^2\big]\,}}, \qquad y := G_A^\top \hat{a}, $$

where $\Delta\Phi$, $\Phi_N$, $C_r$ and $D_r$ are all of dimension $r \times r$, with $r$ the number of cross-correlated frequencies. This makes diagonalisation — needed for the $p$-value calculation — tractable on real data.

$p$-values via generalised $\chi^2$

Under $H_0$, the covariance of the reduced vector $y$ is

$$ \operatorname{Cov}(y) = G_A^\top\big(\phi_N - \Sigma\big)G_A, $$

derived from the distribution of $\delta t$ under the null. Cholesky-whitening $y$, diagonalising the resulting quadratic form $L^\top Q\, L = U^\top \Lambda U$, and writing $z = U\epsilon$ with $\epsilon\sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$, the statistic becomes

$$ \mathcal{D} \;=\; \sum_{i} \lambda_i\, z_i^2, $$

a generalised $\chi^2$ random variable. Its tail probability is then evaluated using Imhof's method, giving the $p$-value

$$ p\,(\mathcal{D} \geq \mathcal{D}_{\rm obs}) \;=\; 1 - \Pr\!\Big(\textstyle\sum_i \lambda_i z_i^2 \le \mathcal{D}_{\rm obs}\Big). $$

Implementation and validation

  • The full Fourier-domain pipeline is implemented in Python and verified on synthetic data against analytic expectations.
  • Equivalence with the enterprise time-domain pipeline was checked numerically across the same datasets.
  • The implementation removes all reliance on nanograv/enterprise by computing the prior covariance $\phi$ natively in the Fourier basis.
  • The pipeline was applied to Fermi-LAT gamma-ray pulsar data as a test case.

Why this matters

Working natively in the Fourier domain renders many ill-conditioned objects — singular white-noise covariance matrices, awkwardly marginalised timing-model parameters — irrelevant to the actual computation, and exposes the underlying low-rank structure of cross-correlations. The result is a cleaner, faster, and numerically more stable evaluation of $p$-values for the optimal statistic.
