Optimising Diamagnetic Levitation

University College London  ·  Supervised by Prof. Peter Barker

A diamagnet is pushed out of strong magnetic fields, so a light enough piece of one floats above a permanent magnet. Pyrolytic graphite is the classic example. My magnet is a cylindrical core magnetised upwards, wrapped tightly in a shell magnetised downwards. The question is how to shape a given piece of graphite, first so that it floats as high as possible, then so that it oscillates sideways as fast as possible.

1The field

There are no free currents, so $\mathbf H$ is the gradient of a scalar potential, and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$ with $\mathbf B = \mu_0(\mathbf H + \mathbf M)$ turns into a Poisson equation sourced by the magnetisation,

$$ \mathbf H = -\nabla\phi_M, \qquad \nabla^2\phi_M = \nabla\cdot\mathbf M. \tag{1} $$

The Green's function of the Laplacian solves it with an integral over the magnet. A uniform magnetisation has no divergence inside, so only the faces contribute, with surface density $\sigma_M = \mathbf M\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}$,

$$ \begin{gathered} \phi_M(\mathbf r) = -\frac{1}{4\pi}\int \frac{\nabla'\cdot\mathbf M(\mathbf r')}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}\,d^3r' = \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint \frac{\sigma_M(\mathbf r')}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}\,dA', \\[4pt] \mathbf B = -\mu_0\nabla\phi_M \quad \text{outside the magnet}. \end{gathered} \tag{2} $$

Core plus shell is twice a cylinder of radius $R_1$ minus a cylinder of radius $R_2$, both magnetised upwards. I evaluate $\mathbf B$ in closed form with elliptic integrals and check it against the quadrature of (2). The two agree to about one part in $10^8$.

2Graphite in the field

For $|\chi| \ll 1$ the graphite picks up the magnetisation $\chi\mathbf B/\mu_0$, and a body $\Omega$ of it has the energy

$$ U = -\frac{\chi}{2\mu_0}\int_\Omega B^2\,dV + \rho g\int_\Omega z\,dV, \qquad \chi < 0. \tag{3} $$

Moving the body is the same as moving the field under it, so the derivative goes inside the integral. Upwards this gives the lift density. Sideways the setup loses its symmetry, so I shift the body by a tiny $\delta$ along $x$ and expand to second order, which after averaging around the axis gives the horizontal stiffness density,

$$ f_z = -\frac{|\chi|}{2\mu_0}\,\partial_z B^2, \qquad \kappa = \frac{|\chi|}{4\mu_0}\Big(\partial_r^2 + \frac{1}{r}\,\partial_r\Big)B^2. \tag{4} $$

The body floats where $\int_\Omega (f_z - \rho g)\,dV = 0$, and sideways it oscillates at

$$ \omega_x^2 = \frac{1}{m}\int_\Omega \kappa\,dV = \frac{1}{V}\int_\Omega w\,dV, \qquad w = \frac{\kappa}{\rho}, \tag{5} $$

where $w$ is the horizontal frequency density. If $\omega_x^2 < 0$, the body slides off.

3Maximum height

Here is the lift density, in units of the weight density $\rho g$. Inside the dashed curve every bit of graphite lifts more than its own weight. A body can stick out of it, as long as the parts inside carry the rest.

The optimisation

For a body whose bottom sits at height $h$, the largest one that still floats solves

$$ \max\,|\Omega| \quad \text{subject to} \quad \int_\Omega (f_z - \rho g)\,dV = 0, \qquad \Omega \subset \{z \ge h\}. $$

A Lagrange multiplier turns the answer into a level set, $\Omega = \{z \ge h,\ f_z \ge \lambda\}$: fill space in order of decreasing lift until the net force falls back to zero. The higher $h$, the smaller this largest body, so for a given mass the maximum height is where the two meet, found by bisection in $h$.

For 200 mg the best shape is a lens-shaped ring from $r = 5.6$ to $11.2$ mm, flat underneath and floating $2.59$ mm above the magnet, with its top along the contour $f_z = 0.88\,\rho g$. Vertically it is stiff, at $12.7$ Hz. Sideways, though, $\omega_x^2 < 0$, and it slides off.

4Horizontal frequency

Now the horizontal frequency density $w$. Green holds a body in place, red pushes it sideways. The red wedges rise from the junction and from the outer edge, where the magnetisation jumps, and the highest floater of Section 3 (dashed) sits right in one.

The optimisation

Maximise $\omega_x^2$ for the same 200 mg, still balanced, with at least 0.25 mm of clearance above the magnet. Fixing the mass matters here, since maximising $\omega_x^2$ alone shrinks the body to a sliver. With the mass and the balance as constraints, the Lagrange conditions again give a level set, now of a mix of stiffness and lift,

$$ \Omega = \Big\{\, w + \nu\Big(\frac{f_z}{\rho g} - 1\Big) \ge c \,\Big\}, $$

with $c$ fixed by the mass and $\nu$ by the balance, both by bisection. The body is kept in one piece, since a ring that straddles a red wedge is pushed off it.

The best single piece hugs the outside of the outer edge: a domed ring from $r = 20.15$ to $21.65$ mm, at most $0.68$ mm thick, sitting $0.25$ mm above the top face. It oscillates sideways at $13.3$ Hz and vertically at $10.8$ Hz, and it is stable against tilting too. Why not over the junction, where the lift is strongest? A piece there gets so much lift that it must carry dead weight to balance, and dead weight adds mass without adding stiffness. At the outer edge the ring is almost self-supporting.

As a check, I displace and tilt both optimal bodies as voxelised 3D objects and differentiate their energy numerically. The forces and stiffnesses agree with the densities (4) to five significant digits.
