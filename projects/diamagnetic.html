Summer Research · 07.2024 – 09.2024

Diamagnetic Levitation

University College London  ·  Supervised by Prof. Peter Barker

A computational physics project on levitating a diamagnetic material above a permanent magnet. The aim was to develop a numerical method that finds the optimal shape of a diamagnetic body — given the geometry and magnetisation of a permanent magnet — that maximises its equilibrium levitation height and the stability of small oscillations about that equilibrium.

Setup

For a body of (negative) magnetic susceptibility $\chi$ in an external field $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})$, the induced magnetisation gives rise to a potential energy

$$ U(\mathbf{r}_0) \;=\; -\,\frac{\chi}{2\mu_0}\int_V \big|\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}_0 + \mathbf{r}')\big|^2 \, d^3 r' \;+\; m g\, z_0 .$$

For $\chi<0$ the body is repelled from regions of high $|\mathbf{B}|^2$. Equilibrium and its stability are governed by the Hessian of $U$ at the levitation point.

Methods

  • Computed $\mathbf{B}$ for a rotationally symmetric source: a uniformly magnetised cylindrical core surrounded by an oppositely magnetised cylindrical shell, taking advantage of axisymmetry to reduce the integral.
  • Discretised the body and used a constrained numerical optimisation over admissible shape functions to maximise the levitation height $z_0$.
  • Linearised the dynamics about the equilibrium, computed the Hessian, and characterised the vertical and horizontal oscillation frequencies as functions of shape.
  • Separately optimised for the shape giving the highest horizontal oscillation frequency, which controls lateral stiffness.
