Research Project · 06.2026 – 10.2026

Self-Force and Gauge Dependence in Black-Hole Scattering

University of Southampton (remote)  ·  Supervised by Prof. Leor Barack

Gravitational self-force

A small body of mass $\mu$ orbiting a black hole of mass $M \gg \mu$ moves, to a first approximation, on a geodesic of the black-hole metric $g_{\mu\nu}$. This approximation ignores the body's own stress–energy: the body neither curves spacetime nor radiates, so its orbit never decays. In reality it emits gravitational waves and slowly spirals in: an extreme-mass-ratio inspiral (EMRI).

Gravitational self-force theory puts the body's stress–energy back, order by order in the mass ratio $\epsilon \sim \mu/M$, and is the most accurate description of EMRIs. At first order the body perturbs the metric, $g_{\mu\nu} \to g_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$, and $h_{\mu\nu}$ solves the linearised Einstein equation sourced by a point particle on the worldline $z^\mu(\tau)$. In Lorenz gauge,

$$ \begin{aligned} \Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} + 2R_{\mu}{}^{\rho}{}_{\nu}{}^{\sigma}\,\bar h_{\rho\sigma} &= -16\pi T_{\mu\nu}, \qquad \nabla^{\nu}\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0, \\ T^{\mu\nu} &= \mu\int u^{\mu}u^{\nu}\,\frac{\delta^{4}\big(x - z(\tau)\big)}{\sqrt{-g}}\,d\tau, \end{aligned} \tag{1} $$

where $\bar h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 g_{\mu\nu}h$ is the trace-reversed perturbation and $u^\mu = dz^\mu/d\tau$.

The perturbation acts back on the body's motion. One cannot simply use the geodesic equation of $g + h$, because the retarded solution $h^{\rm ret}$ diverges on the worldline, like the Coulomb field of a point charge. Detweiler and Whiting split it as $h^{\rm ret} = h^{S} + h^{R}$: the singular field $h^{S}$ is built locally from the worldline and exerts no force, while the regular field $h^{R}$ is a smooth vacuum solution. The body then moves as a test particle in the metric $g + h^{R}$, which to first order is the forced geodesic equation

$$ \begin{aligned} u^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}u^{\mu} &= \mathcal A^{\mu}\big[h^{R}\big] + O(\epsilon^{2}), \\ \mathcal A^{\mu}[h] &= -\tfrac12\left(g^{\mu\nu} + u^{\mu}u^{\nu}\right)\left(2\nabla_{\alpha}h_{\nu\beta} - \nabla_{\nu}h_{\alpha\beta}\right)u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}. \end{aligned} \tag{2} $$

The self-force $\mathcal A^{\mu}[h^{R}]$ contains radiation reaction, which drives the inspiral, and a conservative part, which shifts the orbital frequencies.
