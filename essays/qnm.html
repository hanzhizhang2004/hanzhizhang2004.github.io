Quasinormal Modes of Black Holes

Essay examiner: Prof. Harvey Reall  ·  University of Cambridge

Black holes are remarkably simple in equilibrium. When a black hole is stationary, it is described by only a few parameters, such as its mass, charge and angular momentum. However, its response to perturbations is much richer. A useful analogy is a vibrating string. If a string is disturbed, it responds by oscillating with specific frequencies determined by the system itself. A black hole behaves similarly: after a perturbation, it also undergoes a stage of oscillation before settling down. The difference is that a string with fixed ends is a closed system, whereas a black hole is not. Part of the disturbance can be absorbed by the horizon, and part can propagate away to infinity. The oscillation is therefore damped. We call the stage of damped oscillation the ringdown, and the characteristic modes governing it are called quasinormal modes (QNMs).

In this essay, I review the mathematical structure of QNMs, methods for computing them, and their application to strong cosmic censorship.
